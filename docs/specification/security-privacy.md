# Security, privacy and compliance requirements

These are engineering requirements and review inputs, not a legal opinion or compliance certification. An accountable privacy lead and qualified local counsel must finalize obligations for enabled countries and providers. Global availability should be staged, not assumed from the word global.

The [Master PRD v2](master-prd-v2.md) supersedes an adults-only target product while keeping age-specific capabilities disabled until qualified. [Global policy/guardian architecture](global-policy-architecture.md) and the [companion extension](style-companion-extension.md) add reviewed per-jurisdiction age assurance, verified limited guardian contexts, private editable memory, wardrobe/twin/group isolation, explicit agent confirmations and B2B data/rights boundaries. No inference of ethnicity, religion, gender identity, disability or age from beauty photos; no sensitive profiling or advertising exposure of private profiles. Child photorealistic twins/public profiles and initial youth advertising are excluded. These are proposed expanded controls, not implemented protections or legal certification.

## Data and access requirements

Complete the Look inherits these controls. Private components, matching embeddings and shopping lists are purpose-bound customer data. Commercial ranking receives only approved coarse context and eligible IDs; reports cannot expose private images, facial measurements, inferred skin/hair/body traits, private look IDs or exclusion reasons. Sponsored collections need public-content rights and disclosure. [Commercial rules](look-ranking-commerce.md) define this boundary; [acceptance gates](look-delivery-economics.md) require noninterference, data-contract, reporting and deletion tests. Catalog text is untrusted; model output cannot publish prices, inventory or delivery promises.

1. Classify public product/creator content, private account data, sensitive facial/appearance media and traits, financial records, and security/audit evidence separately. Maintain a purpose/recipient/region/retention map. Facial images are personal data; whether specific processing is legally biometric or special-category data depends on purpose and jurisdiction. Avoid identity recognition and face-template databases altogether.
2. Obtain explicit, granular, revocable consent for capture/analysis, third-party AI processing, saved looks/personalization, and public sharing. Marketing/ad tracking is separate and optional. Present intelligible purposes, processors, retention and limitations before collection. Refusal must not silently enable an alternate processing purpose. Do not bundle consent to use customer media for model training; default is no training and no licensing of customer media.
3. Keep source and generated face imagery in private storage with blocked public ACLs, encrypted transit/at rest and managed keys. Authorize every signed URL issuance; propose 2–5 minute read expiry, narrowly scoped upload expiry, immutable object keys and tenant/owner checks. URLs must not enter analytics, referrers or logs. For immediate revocation requirements, use an authenticated media gateway; already-issued signed URLs may remain usable until expiry. CDN/cache policies must respect privacy and deletion.
4. Strip EXIF/geolocation, enforce decompression/image dimension limits, scan/sanitize uploads in isolation, reject arbitrary fetch URLs and enforce provider egress allowlists. Workers cannot query unrelated users' assets. Restrict object-store IAM by task/prefix and use workload identity rather than long-lived keys.
5. Deny by default using user roles, organization membership, object ownership, purpose and consent. Enforce permissions on the API and asynchronous handlers, not just UI. Admin, finance, moderation, support and reporting roles are separate; require MFA, short privileged sessions, access reviews and audit for exceptional grants. Require dual review for high-value payouts, manual monetary/credit adjustments and provider/privacy-policy changes.
6. Use hosted payment collection/tokenization; no raw card numbers/CVV in platform storage, logs or tests. Establish applicable PCI scope with the PSP; tokenization does not mean no obligations. Protect sessions with PKCE, secure cookie settings for web, CSRF controls, token issuer/audience validation, rotation and revocation. Secure mobile credentials in OS facilities; verify app/store server events independently.
7. Use managed secret storage, workload-scoped service credentials, rotation, encrypted backups and IaC review. Separate production and nonproduction accounts, databases, buckets and keys. Never copy customer facial images into development or evaluation datasets without a separate valid basis and explicit applicable consent.
8. Audit commercial changes and private media access without logging face traits, images, tokens, signed URLs or full webhook/customer payloads. Redact PII at source. Protect audit integrity with restricted append paths and immutable retention exports. Security telemetry uses proportionate pseudonymous signals, not facial identification.

## Retention, rights and consent withdrawal

Proposed defaults: captures/quarantine 24 hours after completion/failure or abandoned upload, unsaved outputs 7 days, saved looks 90 days, retained analysis traits 30 days with explicit personalization consent; users can delete earlier. Purpose-specific scheduled expiry must cover derivatives, thumbnails, caches, warehouse joins and vendors. Saved looks renewal requires user action; do not extend retention simply because an object was read by a service.

Target deletion from active platform storage within 24 hours of an accepted request, provider deletion acknowledgement within the contractual SLA (target 72 hours), and encrypted backup expiry within 35 days. These are requirements to negotiate and verify, not current capabilities. Identity-verified export/deletion requests must meet statutory deadlines; do not demand a selfie merely to process a rights request. Document lawful exceptions for fraud, financial/tax retention or litigation with scoped holds, expiry and accountable approval. Financial records must not retain images for convenience.

Maintain deletion tombstones through backup lifetime. A restore must replay tombstones before enabling user access or jobs. Cancel queued work and prevent publication of outputs whose processing consent was withdrawn. In-flight requests require provider cancellation/deletion where supported, explicit status and documented residual limitations. Deletion evidence includes object-store confirmation, provider result and backup ageing; a deleted database row alone is insufficient.

## Abuse and product safety

Prevent nonconsensual third-party face editing, sexual exploitation, impersonation and deceptive commercial endorsement. Require rights/consent confirmation for submitted faces and references; block prohibited transformations, provide reporting/appeal, and maintain trained human review with controlled evidence access. A confirmation checkbox alone is not sufficient mitigation; combine rate limits, content checks, account controls and investigation. Do not make dermatology diagnoses, guaranteed fit claims or medical recommendations. Qualify product claims, disclose simulations, and label paid placements prominently.

Protect commerce against fake salons, counterfeit products, affiliate self-dealing, coupon abuse, ad click fraud, account takeover and payout redirection. Use business verification, payout change cooling periods, risk review, velocity limits, connected-account verification, signed callbacks and reversible financial holds through licensed PSP mechanisms. Log decisions and offer appeal where appropriate. Fraud tools cannot consume facial analysis data for ad optimization.

## Incident and assurance requirements

Threat-model capture/upload, provider dispatch, object authorization, organization switching, commercial webhooks, payouts and ad-report queries. Conduct independent penetration testing before paid launch and after material trust-boundary changes. Use dependency/SBOM scanning and signed build provenance, minimal container images, least privilege, patch SLAs and rollback drills. Monitor anomalous object access, privilege grants, secret exposure, ledger exceptions and deletion failures.

Assign on-call ownership and incident severity, isolate affected features, preserve redacted evidence, rotate/revoke access, and notify affected users/regulators within applicable legal deadlines. Test an image-exposure incident and a payment inconsistency incident before launch. Do not claim SOC 2, ISO certification, POPIA/GDPR compliance or production security from this document alone.
