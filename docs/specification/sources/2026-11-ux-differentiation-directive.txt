# GLAM AI — UX Review: How to Make It Stand Apart

This is a significant improvement over the earlier design. The website now communicates AI beauty analysis rather than fashion shopping, and the desktop and mobile layouts share a coherent premium identity.

But I would make one major change before approving the UX:

GLAM AI should feel less like a beautiful website with AI features and more like a personal beauty and styling assistant that immediately understands what the user wants to achieve.

## 1. The biggest issue: mobile users must scroll before discovering the real product

In your mobile screenshot, the hero photograph occupies much of the initial screen, followed by a large headline and two buttons. The actual Face Analysis and Color Analysis cards are partly hidden behind the bottom navigation.

The photography is excellent for branding, but it delays the action users came for.

I would restructure the mobile experience like this:

✧ GLAM AI

YOUR PERSONAL AI STYLIST

# What would you like to explore today?

Analyze My Features Ask My Personal Stylist

Find My Colors

Try a Makeover

Style an Event

Complete a Look

Your GLAM Journey

Your analyses, saved looks, styling plans and recommended products appear here once you begin.

Home

Analyze

Studio

Advisor

Profile

This puts the main actions in the first screen rather than below the fold.

I would retain the large editorial photograph on desktop, but reduce its dominance on mobile.

## 3. The feature I believe could differentiate GLAM AI most: one continuous styling conversation

Imagine a user tells GLAM AI:

> I'm attending a wedding in Cape Town next month. I want something elegant, modest, emerald green, under R4,000. I also need a hairstyle that suits me.

Instead of sending the user through separate Face Analysis, Color Analysis, AI Studio, Fashion and Shop pages, GLAM AI should orchestrate the whole experience.

Your AI Personal Stylist

I can help you create a complete look for your wedding. Would you like ready-made outfits, a custom design from a tailor, or both?

Ready-madeCustom tailorCompare both

Your styling plan

Outfit concept · Hairstyle · Makeup · Accessories · Where to buy · Tailor options · Budget

Illustrative experience. Real recommendations would require verified merchant inventory and availability.

The assistant should remember the context of the styling session and update the complete look whenever the user changes a preference.

That is a stronger experience than a collection of unrelated AI generators.

## 4. Six UX features I would add

| Feature                    | Benefit                                                                                           |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Persistent GLAM Profile    | Saves voluntarily provided sizes, budgets, style preferences, analysis results and wardrobe items |
| Live Look Builder          | Lets users change hairstyle, makeup, clothing and accessories in one coordinated look             |
| Complete Look Budget       | Displays total cost, including products, tailoring, shipping and services                         |
| Compare Looks Side by Side | Makes choosing between AI-generated looks easier                                                  |
| One-Tap Recreate           | Turns a saved look into tutorials, exact merchant listings and booking options                    |
| Contextual AI Advisor      | Lets users ask questions directly from an analysis report, makeover or product listing            |

I would especially prioritize Live Look Builder.

A user should be able to change a hairstyle without losing their outfit, replace a dress without restarting their makeup recommendations, and see how those choices affect the overall budget.

## 6. Role switching should be visible but unobtrusive

The desktop and mobile screenshots currently show a profile icon. That is the right location for a workspace switcher, but business users need a clear indication of which workspace is active.

For example, after a merchant switches roles, the header could display:

GLAM AI · Merchant Workspace

And the mobile bottom navigation would become:

Dashboard | Snap & List | Products | Orders | Profile

A creator, advertiser, tailor or salon owner would see their corresponding navigation.

The consumer's AI beauty experience should remain completely separate visually from business management.

## 8. Exact instruction for Codex

GLAM AI — Final UX Differentiation Directive

# GLAM AI — Final UX Differentiation Directive

Retain the existing premium visual identity but redesign the experience around user goals rather than disconnected AI tools.

## Core positioning

GLAM AI is a personal AI beauty, grooming and styling companion that helps users understand their features, discover suitable looks, recreate inspiration and obtain the exact products or professional services needed.

## Mobile UX

* Reduce the oversized homepage hero image.

* Make Face Analysis and AI Personal Stylist immediately accessible above the fold.

* Preserve navigation: Home, Analyze, AI Studio, AI Advisor, Profile.

* Keep the bottom navigation fixed and account for safe-area insets.

* Avoid content being obscured by the bottom navigation.

* Provide clear first-use onboarding and returning-user dashboards.

## Goal-driven entry points

Implement:

1. Understand What Suits Me.

2. Style Me for an Event.

3. Recreate This Look.

4. Try a New Hairstyle or Makeup Look.

5. Style My Existing Wardrobe.

6. Find Products and Professionals.

## Conversational Personal Stylist

Allow users to describe goals naturally, upload inspiration and event invitations, refine recommendations through conversation, and maintain context across the styling journey.

## Live Look Builder

Allow users to combine and modify hairstyle, makeup, clothing, accessories and professional services within one saved look.

Changing one component must not erase unrelated selections.

## Get This Look

Every relevant analysis, makeover and styling result must provide an actionable path to matching merchant listings, professional bookings or custom-tailoring quotes.

Display verified availability, prices, variants, delivery estimates, merchant identity and total cost where available.

Support the established 10% platform commission on completed item sales.

## Persistent GLAM Profile

Save user-approved analysis reports, style preferences, wardrobe items, saved looks and shopping preferences.

Make saved data editable and deletable.

Do not infer religious identity or other sensitive characteristics from photographs.

## Workspace switching

Support Personal, Creator, Professional, Merchant, Advertiser and Affiliate workspaces through one identity and organization-scoped RBAC.

## Quality gates

Before approving the redesign, produce interactive mobile and desktop prototypes for:

* First-time visitor.

* First face analysis.

* Returning user.

* Event styling.

* AI makeover.

* Live Look Builder.

* Get This Look.

* Merchant Snap & List.

* Workspace switching.

Test task completion, clarity, accessibility, loading states, mobile safe areas and real product-matching accuracy.

Do not claim live AI analysis, product inventory, payments or merchant bookings until those integrations are verified.

My overall recommendation: Keep the visual design, but make GLAM AI's defining interaction a single continuous journey from a personal goal to a complete achievable look.

That is the experience I would prioritize over adding more homepage sections, more AI tools or more shopping categories. It would give GLAM AI a clearer identity—and a stronger reason for users to return—than simply offering face scans and makeover filters.
