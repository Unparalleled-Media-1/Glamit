GLAM AI — Complete AI-Builder Product Specification

Master Product Requirements Document • Version 1.0 • November 2026

Product: AI Beauty, Grooming, Makeover & Personal Styling Platform
Target market: Global, with African specialization
Platforms: iOS, Android and web-based administration
Development approach: AI-assisted development using Emergent or a comparable builder
Business model: Freemium subscriptions, AI credits, beauty commerce and marketplace commissions

This specification is designed to guide the development of a production-ready competitor to Glam Up, rather than a demonstration app.

The central product promise is:

Discover your best look, visualize it realistically, learn how to achieve it, and find the products and professionals who can help.

The application must support diverse skin tones, facial features, hair textures, cultural aesthetics and beauty preferences. African specialization must be a core product capability, not a separate, lower-quality experience.

The working name GLAM AI is provisional. Trademark, domain and app-store name availability must be checked before committing to it.

Suggested pricing: Plus at $4.99/month or $34.99/year; Pro at $9.99/month or $69.99/year. Configure local prices through App Store Connect and Google Play rather than performing a simplistic currency conversion at checkout.

Credit architecture

Separate subscription entitlements from consumable generation credits.

A credit ledger should record the source of credits, reservation, consumption, refund, expiry where applicable, and a unique transaction reference. Credit balances must be derived from authorized ledger transactions, not editable directly by the client.

For example, a standard makeover could cost one render credit and an advanced multi-look generation three credits. Show the exact amount before submission.

Purchased consumable credits and subscription-included credits should be tracked separately, with transparent usage priority and expiry policies. Refunds, renewals, upgrades, downgrades, grace periods and chargebacks must all have explicit server-side rules.

Additional monetization

In Phase 2, introduce affiliate product links with clear commercial disclosure. In Phase 3, introduce professional booking commissions, potentially starting at 10–15% as a commercial hypothesis to test.

Do not assume that digital subscriptions and real-world salon services follow identical Apple or Google payment rules. Each payment flow needs its own store-policy review.

8. Security, privacy and responsible AI

The following requirements are release blockers, not optional enhancements.

Identity and access: Secure authentication, server-side RBAC, signed media URLs, session revocation, rate limiting and protection against account enumeration.

Media protection: Private storage, encryption in transit and at rest, EXIF metadata stripping where appropriate, upload validation, short-lived access URLs and configurable deletion schedules.

AI privacy: Explicit disclosure of external AI processors, documented data processing arrangements, restrictions on model training using customer images, and no biometric identity matching.

Consumer protection: Transparent subscriptions, visible renewal terms, cancellation instructions, accessible support and clear refund handling.

Responsible recommendations: Avoid medical diagnosis, harmful appearance judgments, unsupported product efficacy claims and misleading promises about cosmetic results.

Global compliance: Design for POPIA and applicable GDPR, UK GDPR and other regional privacy obligations. Legal counsel should review the actual processing activities and launch territories before release.

Children: The initial product is adult-focused. Implement age eligibility controls and do not introduce child-specific appearance scoring or cosmetic-pressure features.

Operational reliability

Target 99.9% monthly availability for the core application API after stabilization, with separately measured AI-provider availability.

Use managed database backups, tested restoration procedures, infrastructure-as-code, alerting, error budgets and documented incident response. Production secrets must never be stored in the mobile app or repository.

8. One further commercial opportunity

Given your existing Dress 4 Less business, there is a potential advantage in having a real fashion inventory available to the platform rather than relying entirely on external affiliate merchants.

For example, the AI stylist could recommend a complete outfit and link directly to matching Dress 4 Less products. That could provide an initial merchant catalog while the platform recruits additional fashion sellers.

However, I would keep the beauty platform commercially independent and allow competing fashion retailers to participate. Otherwise, restricting product recommendations to one retailer could reduce the platform's long-term appeal to users and advertisers.

Proposed weighting for sponsored placement ranking, not a trained recommendation model. Eligibility, safety, stock and relevance are mandatory filters before bidding is considered.

7. The strategic opportunity

This makes the five businesses reinforce one another:

AI creates the inspiration → users try the look → advertisers supply the products → professionals deliver the services → creators drive discovery → GLAM AI earns from the resulting transactions.

I would make Complete the Look the flagship commercial feature and prioritize it ahead of a general-purpose advertising feed.

One additional feature could be especially powerful for African markets: Complete My Look Within My Budget. A user could enter R500, R1,500 or R5,000, and the platform would assemble the closest achievable hairstyle, makeup and outfit combination from actual available offers.

That gives users a practical reason to return and gives advertisers access to customers who are already expressing purchase intent—without requiring the platform to manufacture that intent through intrusive advertising.
