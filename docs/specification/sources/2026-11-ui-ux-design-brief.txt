# GLAM AI — Complete UI/UX Design Brief

Mobile Applications + Web Platform | Global Product | Design Specification v1.0

# Every Look. Every Style. Everyone.

Product vision: Design a premium, inclusive, AI-powered beauty, grooming, fashion and lifestyle platform where users can discover a look, try it virtually, find the products needed to recreate it, shop from relevant merchants and book professionals.

Primary experience: Discover → Try → Personalize → Complete the Look → Shop / Book → Share.

The design must feel like a combination of a premium fashion editorial, an intuitive AI creative studio and a seamless shopping experience—not a cluttered super-app.

The product will serve all genders, ethnicities, skin tones, hair textures, body types, abilities, budgets and cultures globally. Age-specific experiences must be designed with appropriate protections.

## Brand personality

GLAM AI should feel:

* Premium but accessible: Fashion-editorial quality without excluding affordable shoppers.

* Inclusive: Real representation of different people and personal styles.

* Intelligent: AI functionality feels useful and effortless.

* Expressive: Bold imagery, elegant typography and customizable style experiences.

* Trustworthy: Clear prices, sponsorship labels, privacy controls and reliable shopping information.

Use warm ivory backgrounds, deep charcoal text, restrained rose accents and occasional gold for premium collections. Lilac may be used for AI-specific elements. Never use color alone to indicate meaning.

Typography should pair an expressive editorial serif for large campaign headlines with a highly legible modern sans-serif for navigation, forms and product information. The interface must support international scripts and right-to-left layouts.

# 6. Responsive design and accessibility

The design system should use shared components and tokens across mobile and web while respecting each platform's native interaction patterns.

| Device        | UX approach                                                   |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Small mobile  | Single-column, bottom navigation, full-screen AI preview      |
| Large mobile  | Larger cards, flexible grids, accessible controls             |
| Tablet        | Two-column layouts, split-view studio                         |
| Desktop       | Multi-column workspaces, persistent filters and comparisons   |
| Large desktop | Wider editorial imagery, efficient data tables and dashboards |

Target WCAG 2.2 AA where applicable. Include scalable typography, keyboard navigation, screen-reader labels, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion support, accessible touch targets and right-to-left localization.

# 7. Critical UX principles

1. Show value before the paywall. Let users explore looks and understand the product before being asked to subscribe.

2. Make AI progress transparent. Display queued, processing, completed and failed states with retry options.

3. Preserve user identity. Do not automatically lighten skin, reshape bodies or alter facial characteristics.

4. Keep shopping honest. Never invent product prices, stock or delivery promises.

5. Separate sponsored recommendations. Commercial placement must be clearly disclosed.

6. Design for all ages safely. Teen and child experiences require appropriate feature restrictions.

7. Protect privacy by default. Selfies and generated looks remain private unless explicitly shared.

8. Reduce friction. A user should be able to go from inspiration to a shoppable look in a small number of clear steps.

9. Support low-bandwidth markets. Optimize images, provide progressive loading and avoid unnecessary downloads.

10. Use consistent design patterns. Mobile, web, merchant and admin experiences should feel like one product family.

# 8. Design deliverables required before development

The UI/UX team should produce a complete Figma design system and screen library covering:

* Brand identity, typography, color tokens, icons and component states.

* Consumer mobile app screens and clickable prototypes.

* Consumer desktop and tablet layouts.

* Merchant, advertiser, professional and creator portals.

* Admin dashboard.

* Onboarding, AI Studio, Complete the Look, checkout/handoff and booking journeys.

* Empty, loading, error, offline, permission-denied and success states.

* Accessibility and localization variants.

* Design-to-development specifications for Codex.

* Usability test scenarios and acceptance criteria.

Every important screen must be linked to a user journey and backend capability. Do not approve attractive mockups that cannot support real product workflows.

# GLAM AI — Global Mobile & Web UI/UX Design Brief

You are the lead product designer and design systems architect for GLAM AI.

Design a premium, inclusive, global AI-powered beauty, grooming, fashion and lifestyle commerce platform.

## Product positioning

Tagline: Every Look. Every Style. Everyone.

The platform allows users to discover styles, upload inspiration, try looks virtually, personalize them, identify the products and professionals required, compare offers, shop, book services and share collections.

## Platforms

Design the following connected applications:

1. iOS consumer application.

2. Android consumer application.

3. Responsive consumer website.

4. Merchant web portal.

## Design direction

Create an editorial luxury aesthetic that remains accessible to everyday shoppers.

Use warm ivory, charcoal, restrained rose and complementary accent colors. Prioritize large, authentic lifestyle photography, clean typography, whitespace, polished cards and subtle motion.

Do not copy an existing app's proprietary layouts, illustrations or brand assets.

## Mandatory user experiences

* Inclusive onboarding and optional Style DNA.

* Visual inspiration discovery.

* AI hairstyle, makeup and fashion try-on.

* Screenshot to Shop.

* Complete the Look.

* Budget-based styling and shopping.

* Occasion Concierge.

* Product and merchant comparison.

* Beauty professional booking and quotation.

* Creator storefronts and shoppable looks.

* Subscription and AI credit management.

* Transparent sponsored recommendations.

* Privacy, consent and accessibility controls.

## Mobile navigation

Home, Discover, Studio, Shop, Profile.

Provide contextual access to the AI Advisor, saved looks, shopping lists and notifications.

## Web navigation

Discover, AI Studio, Hair & Beauty, Fashion, Shop the Look, Professionals and Creators.

Optimize desktop for visual browsing, comparison and detailed AI editing.

## Commercial dashboard requirements

Create role-specific dashboards for merchants, advertisers, professionals, creators and administrators.

Advertiser tools must support campaign creation, sponsored placement previews, budgets, bids, performance reporting and billing.

Merchant tools must support product catalogs, inventory, orders/leads, promotions and analytics.

## Inclusivity

Represent a broad range of ethnicities, ages, genders, body types, hair textures, disabilities and cultural styles.

Do not infer protected or sensitive identities from photographs.

Design age-appropriate experiences and prevent appearance-ranking features.

## UX quality standards

* Responsive across supported screen sizes.

* Accessible interactions and typography.

* Consistent reusable components.

* Clear AI loading and failure states.

* Transparent prices and sponsored labels.

* Private-by-default media.

* Realistic checkout and booking flows.

* Localization and RTL support.

* Graceful behavior under slow network conditions.

## Required deliverables

Before implementation, produce:

1. Information architecture for every application.

2. Complete screen inventory.

3. User journey maps.

4. Low-fidelity wireframes.

5. High-fidelity Figma designs.

6. Responsive design variants.

7. Reusable component library and design tokens.

8. Interactive prototypes of critical flows.

9. Empty, loading, error and success states.

10. Accessibility audit checklist.

11. Design-to-code specifications.

12. Screen-by-screen acceptance criteria.

Prioritize the complete Discover → Try → Complete the Look → Shop / Book journey.

Do not begin implementation until the primary flows and design system are approved.

My recommendation: Have the designer complete the mobile consumer experience and the desktop Complete the Look flow first. Once those are approved, use the same design system for the merchant, advertiser, professional, creator and admin portals.

That will establish a coherent visual identity while ensuring the platform's most important commercial journey is designed before Codex begins building it.
