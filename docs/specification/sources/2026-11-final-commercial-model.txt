# GLAM AI — Final Commercial Model & Missing Critical Features

Agreed. GLAM AI will charge a 10% platform commission on every item sold through the platform. This should be built into the central commerce and settlement engine, not implemented separately for each merchant category.

I would also make the commission consistent across consumer mobile, web, Snap & List products, Complete the Look, AI Personal Stylist and event outfit recommendations.

## 1. GLAM AI's 10% commission model

The commercial policy should be:

* 10% commission on every completed item sale through GLAM AI, including organically recommended and sponsored products.

* Commission calculated on the final eligible item value after discounts, excluding separately charged delivery and taxes unless the commercial policy explicitly defines otherwise.

* Cancelled transactions incur no earned commission; refunds and partial refunds reverse the corresponding commission.

* The merchant sees the fee before accepting the transaction.

* Advertising fees are separate from the 10% sales commission.

* No duplicate commission if the same item appears in several recommendation journeys.

One important distinction: if GLAM AI sends a buyer to an external retailer's checkout, the platform cannot automatically deduct 10%. That requires a retailer agreement, attributable order reporting and settlement or invoicing. To guarantee collection, GLAM AI needs an integrated checkout or contracted marketplace-payment arrangement.

For tailor and salon services, I would keep the 10% product-sale rule fixed and explicitly decide whether service bookings also attract 10%, rather than silently extending an item-sale policy to services.

## 5. Final Codex instruction

# GLAM AI — Final Commerce Policy and Missing Feature Requirements

## Mandatory commercial policy

GLAM AI charges a fixed 10% platform commission on every completed item sale processed through the platform.

Apply the same commission policy across mobile, web, AI analysis recommendations, Complete the Look, Personal Stylist, event styling, merchant catalogs and Snap & List products.

Calculate commission from the eligible final item selling price, after item discounts, excluding separately stated delivery charges and applicable taxes unless a formally approved policy specifies otherwise.

Support:

* Multi-merchant carts and orders.

* Accurate commission allocation per order line.

* Merchant settlement and payout reconciliation.

* Partial refunds and corresponding commission reversals.

* Chargebacks and disputes.

* Merchant transaction statements.

* Platform financial reporting.

* Audit logs and idempotent payment processing.

* Country-specific taxes and payment requirements.

* Separately accounted advertising fees.

Do not assume external affiliate checkouts automatically provide GLAM AI with 10% commission. Require a contracted attribution and settlement arrangement.

Service booking commissions must remain separately configurable until approved.

## Additional required capabilities

1. Integrated marketplace checkout.

2. Merchant identity and business verification.

3. Order, return, refund and dispute management.

4. Product authenticity and safety moderation.

5. Inventory synchronization and availability verification.

6. AI size and fit recommendations.

7. Digital wardrobe and owned-product library.

8. Event styling with deadline-aware sourcing.

9. Custom design-to-tailor quotation and production tracking.

10. Wishlist, restock and price-drop alerts.

11. Verified product reviews.

12. Creator storefronts and attributable sales.

13. Persistent, consent-based personal stylist preferences.

14. Financial fraud monitoring and commission reconciliation.

15. Unified analytics covering analysis-to-purchase conversion.

## Architecture

Use one canonical product catalog, recommendation engine, order system, commission ledger and merchant settlement layer.

All consumer and business workspaces must share the same underlying services with organization-scoped RBAC.

Maintain privacy boundaries between consumer facial-analysis data and advertiser targeting.

## Release controls

Before launch, test the full flow:

AI Analysis → Product Recommendation → Verified Merchant Listing → Cart → Payment → Order → Commission → Merchant Settlement → Refund or Return.

Block production release if any financial, privacy, authorization or settlement control is unverified.

Do not represent mocked payment, inventory, shipping or merchant integrations as live.

Produce a final gap analysis and phased backlog before implementing additional modules.

## 6. My recommendation before freezing the build

I would classify the product into three release stages:

| Release                        | Scope                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Release 1 — Core GLAM AI       | Face, hair and color analysis; AI makeover; personal stylist; event styling; merchant Snap & List; product recommendations; checkout; 10% commission; essential security and operations |
| Release 2 — Marketplace Growth | Virtual wardrobe, designer/tailor workflows, professional bookings, creator storefronts, advanced advertising and merchant analytics                                                    |
| Release 3 — Global Scale       | Enterprise AI APIs, international merchant integrations, advanced sizing, regional expansion and sophisticated advertising optimization                                                 |

One final commercial decision is needed: Should the same 10% commission apply to completed salon, makeup artist, tailor and fashion designer service bookings, or only to physical products?

My recommendation is to apply 10% to both completed product sales and completed service bookings for simplicity, while keeping subscriptions and advertising fees separate. But the service-booking rate should be explicitly approved before Codex hardcodes it.
