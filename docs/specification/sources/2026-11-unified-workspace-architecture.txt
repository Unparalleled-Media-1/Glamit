However, I would distinguish between two things:

Account switching: Moving between personal and business identities.

Role switching: Changing which authorized workspace or business function is active.

Both should be supported, but they must not create duplicate user accounts.

5. Role activation and verification

Users should not receive commercial privileges merely because they select a workspace.

Personal account created: Consumer features become available according to age and consent settings.

Apply for a business role: Choose Creator, Professional, Merchant, Advertiser or Affiliate.

Provide required information: Business details, portfolio, contact information and relevant verification documents.

Verification: Automated checks and manual review where appropriate.

Role activated: The workspace appears in the switcher with approved permissions.

Additional team members: Organization owners invite employees and assign restricted roles.

A user can request multiple roles. Approval, suspension or revocation of one business role should not automatically disable their unrelated personal account unless platform-wide safety rules require it.

6. How to prevent the app becoming cluttered

This is the most important UI/UX rule.

The default experience remains GLAM AI's personal beauty and face-analysis application.

Business tools should not appear throughout the consumer interface.

For example:

A personal user sees Face Analysis, Color Analysis, AI Studio and Glow-Up Guides.

A creator sees content publishing and earnings only after switching to Creator.

A salon owner sees bookings and clients only in Professional.

An advertiser sees campaigns and analytics only in Advertiser.

The app remembers the last active workspace, but first-time users start in Personal.

Cross-workspace notifications can appear in one inbox with clear organization labels, while respecting role permissions and age restrictions.
